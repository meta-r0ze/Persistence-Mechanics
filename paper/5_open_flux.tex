\section{Open Systems: Necessity Without Additivity}
\label{sec:open_systems}
Many persistent systems (cells, firms, ecosystems) are open, exchanging energy and information. But the necessity and sufficiency proofs of \cref{sec:architecture_of_persistence,sec:dynamics_of_persistence} were established strictly within QSS. We show necessity of the six pillars survives unchanged; only the Entropic Balance Sheet (\cref{subsec:entropic_balance_sheet}) requires re-derivation in flux-driven capacity.

\subsection{Pointwise Necessity and Operational Latency}
\label{subsec:necessity_flux_independent}
Because the substrate continuously filters for causal coherence, survival is an instantaneous requirement, not a time-averaged one.

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise Necessity]
\label{prop:pointwise_necessity}
For any system persisting past instant $t$, all six pillars must be structurally instantiated at $t$, regardless of external flux $\mathrm{d}_eS(t)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the exponential filter (\cref{eq:survival_prob}), survival over a history $\Gamma$ is multiplicative. If any pillar is functionally absent during an arbitrarily small interval $\mathrm{d}t$, the local survival probability drops to zero ($q_t \to 0$) via the failure modes of \cref{subsec:pillars_necessity}. The entire multiplicative history collapses. The requirement is therefore instantaneous and flux-independent.
\end{proof}

Flux alters a pillar's \emph{operation}, not its \emph{existence}. During phases of rapid growth or unbounded resource influx, a constraint mechanism might be operationally latent ($f_{\mathrm{GOV}} \approx 0$), incurring no active coordination cost. Yet its \emph{structural capacity} must exist at $t$; if the mechanism is fundamentally absent, the system fails to persist the moment it reaches environmental saturation.


\subsection{Open Systems and the Dynamic Budget}
\label{subsec:open_systems_dynamic_budget}
Strict additivity ($Z_{\mathrm{net}} = \sum_i Z_i$) relies on the Quiescent Steady State, where orthogonal domains prevent interference. In open systems, external flux raises the dissipation ceiling; as operations scale against this moving ceiling, resource contention forces non-linear cross-coupling $\Delta(t)$ between previously orthogonal pillars:

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:open_balance}
    Z_{\mathrm{net}}(t) = \sum_i Z_i(t) + \Delta(t).
\end{equation}

A stochastic derivation of $\Delta(t)$ from substrate-native properties is developed in \cref{app:dynamic_budget}; three regimes emerge as its limits, where $\rho$ is the dimensionless coordination load:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Onsager Recovery (Low $\rho$):} $\Delta \to 0$; QSS additivity is the leading-order behavior.
    \item \textbf{The Thrashing Asymptote (High $\rho$):} Coordination cost diverges as the dissipation ceiling is approached, forcing structural pauses (apoptosis, circuit breakers) to shed load.
    \item \textbf{Kingman's Formula (Non-Markovian Demand):} Relaxing the memoryless assumption recovers the standard
    heavy-traffic generalization, with the $\rho/(1-\rho)$ form as its memoryless special case.
\end{enumerate}
The derivation does not alter the necessity claim. The six pillars remain structurally mandatory regardless of the specific form of $\Delta(t)$; the idealized limit where $\Delta(t) \to 0$ recovers QSS additivity.

It also gives these three regimes what they have historically lacked: a single origin. Linear-response recovery, heavy-traffic divergence, and the correlated-demand generalization were each discovered independently, in unrelated fields, from field-specific premises. Here they emerge as three limits of one substrate function.
