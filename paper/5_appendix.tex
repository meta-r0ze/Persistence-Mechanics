\section{An Example of the Dynamic Budget via Queueing-Theoretic Contention}
\label{app:dynamic_budget}

Open systems violate the orthogonality that made $\bar{Z}_{\mathrm{net}}$ strictly additive in QSS. To quantify the resulting contention between Protocol and Governor, we derive the cross-coupling $\Delta(t)$ in \cref{eq:open_balance} from discrete substrate properties.

\subsection{Coordination capacity and load}
Protocol and Governor share the substrate that performs all state transitions. The Margolus-Levitin limit bounds the minimum time between distinguishable operations, yielding a substrate-level coordination rate $f_{\max}$. In QSS, the two pillars operate on orthogonal domains (their demands share no microstate), so their loads add. With $f_{\mathrm{PRO}} + f_{\mathrm{GOV}} \ll f_{\max}$, cycles remain effectively empty and orthogonality holds ($\Delta = 0$). The dimensionless coordination load is therefore
\begin{equation}
    \rho(t) = \frac{f_{\text{PRO}}(t) + f_{\text{GOV}}(t)}{f_{\text{max}}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Temporal independence and the geometric waiting distribution}
The load above governs instantaneous rates, not waiting-time statistics. We model retry behavior as a memoryless process: each cycle presents pending events symmetrically, with service probability $1-\rho$ per event. This is a modeling hypothesis about temporal independence, not a consequence of orthogonality. The waiting time follows a geometric distribution with mean
\begin{equation}
    \langle k \rangle = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} k \, \rho^k(1-\rho) = \frac{\rho}{1-\rho}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The coordination cross-term}
Each pending cycle incurs the fixed toll $c_0$ (the dimensionless Landauer scale; on thermal substrates this projects to $k_B T \ln 2$). The total waiting cost for both streams is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\text{total}}(t) = \frac{c_0 \, f_{\max} \, \rho}{1-\rho}.
\end{equation}

The QSS baseline (\cref{eq:entropic_balance}) already prices the linear coordination costs $Z_{\mathrm{PRO}} + Z_{\mathrm{GOV}} \approx c_0(f_{\mathrm{PRO}} + f_{\mathrm{GOV}})$ at $\rho \ll 1$. The cross-coupling $\Delta(t)$ is the excess beyond this baseline:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta(t) = \Delta_{\text{total}}(t) - c_0 \, f_{\max} \, \rho = \frac{c_0 \, f_{\max} \, \rho^2}{1-\rho}.
\end{equation}

This recovers the three regimes of \cref{subsec:open_systems_dynamic_budget}: linear-response recovery as $\rho \to 0$, saturation divergence as $\rho \to 1$, and correlated-demand behavior under heavy-tailed waiting distributions.

\subsection{Scope and validity}
This construction assumes discrete cycles (the Margolus-Levitin bound) and symmetric, memoryless retry. Strongly correlated, heavy-tailed, or long-range-dependent arrivals fall outside its scope.
